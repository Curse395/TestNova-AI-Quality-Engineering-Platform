Checkout Flow Overview

The checkout flow is the final and most critical step of the purchasing journey. It begins when a customer adds items to the cart and proceeds to the payment screen. On the payment screen, the customer can choose from multiple payment methods such as credit card, debit card, or digital wallets. After selecting a payment method, the customer enters their shipping address and contact information. The application then validates the address, applies any applicable taxes and shipping fees, and presents a final order summary. The customer reviews the summary and confirms the order. Once confirmed, the backend creates the order, reserves inventory, and sends a confirmation email. Common failure points include expired payment tokens, invalid coupon codes, and network timeouts during the payment provider callback. Automated tests should cover the happy path, the retry path, and the error path where the payment provider returns a declined response. The quality team should also verify that the order is not created twice when the customer double-clicks the confirm button, and that partial failures do not leave the cart in an inconsistent state.

Integration with the loyalty program and gift cards is also important. The system must apply discounts in the correct order and recalculate totals whenever the cart changes. Edge cases such as empty cart, out of stock items, and address validation failures should be included in the regression suite. The goal is to ensure that the checkout experience is fast, reliable, and consistent across all supported devices and browsers.
